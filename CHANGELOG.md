# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Made the log record an error the game catches from the mod, once for each fault. It went only to the game's own `console.txt` before. Wrote the first person frame time to the log once a minute, down from every five seconds, so a long session leaves a log the other lines can be found in.
- Made the mod stay out of the game after a game update that renames or removes something it uses, so the game runs without it. The log names what changed and ends with `the mod stays out of the game:`.
- Added `FieldOfView` to `CameraUnlock.ini`: the first person view's field of view in degrees from the bottom of the picture to the top, `65` by default and `40` to `110`. The game has no field of view setting of its own. The mod reads it again while the game runs, so a change shows within a second or so of saving the file, and the reticle, the weapon in your hands and head tracking all follow it. Aiming a firearm still narrows the view by the same ratio.
- Added a settings file, `CameraUnlock.ini`, which the mod creates next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe` the first time it starts. It holds the tracker's UDP port, the two smoothing values, the lean limits, the wall check, the carried light's multiplier and the hotkeys, which can now be rebound. A setting left on `default` takes its value from `%AppData%\CameraUnlock\Defaults.ini`, which every head tracking mod that keeps its settings in `CameraUnlock.ini` reads.
- Made the mod try the tracker's UDP port again every half second if it cannot be opened when the game starts, for instance because another game with a head tracking mod is still running. It starts listening when the port is free, with no restart. The log records the reason the port could not be opened.
- Saved the tracking mode (`Page Up`) and the yaw mode (`Page Down`) when changed, as the aim mode already was. `End` still lasts only until the game closes.
- Made the installer put `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.dll` next to the jar. It reads and writes the settings file.
- Added four ways of aiming down a firearm's sights with head tracking on, cycled on `Insert` / `Ctrl+Shift+U` and saved when changed: sights locked (the default), free look with a marker, true free look and stock sights. In sights locked a lean across the aim moves the weapon and where the shot starts with your eye, so the sights stay lined up and the shot lands where they point. In the free look modes the weapon stays put and your head moves round it. In stock sights your head's turn and lean ease out while the sights are up and only its tilt stays.
- Kept the weapon in your hands on your aim when you turn or lean your head, and pointed its barrel at the place the shot will land at the hip.
- Kept the left hand on a firearm's fore-end. It used to follow the game's own animation, and flew off the weapon, dragged its sleeve across the view or vanished when you turned or walked.
- Joined the hands on a held weapon to their sleeves with a wrist that bends as a wrist does, at the hip, with the sights up and through a swing. Each hand is turned on the grip to meet the arm behind it, the forearm comes up to the grip from below, and the cuff is the shape of the wrist and closes over it.
- Let leaning in reach the full 0.40 m at the hip, and stopped it just short of the rear sight with the sights up.
- Held a weapon before the eye in first person. A firearm sits low and to the right at the hip and comes up to the middle of the picture when the aim is held; a melee weapon is held up at the lower right and its swing is seen from the eye. The arms that hold it come with it. The weapon trails a turn of the view, sways with each step, drops while sprinting and dips on a crouch or a landing.
- Made a shot in first person kick the weapon back and up, throw a flash from its muzzle and light the people and zombies near it for the length of the flash. Where the shot goes and what it hits are unchanged.
- Drew a small crosshair where the shot would land in first person, with a weapon in hand and the aim not held. The game's own reticle takes over while the aim is held, as before.
- Added a pointer to the first person view. The inventory key shows the inventory and loot panels and hands the mouse back as a pointer, and the same key or `Escape` puts them away and returns to the look. The pointer also comes out by itself while a window, dialog or menu of the game's is open, and for as long as the middle mouse button is held. The picture, head tracking and the game carry on meanwhile.
- Added a first person view. `F8` switches between it and the game's own view: the mouse turns the camera, the movement keys follow it, and the world is drawn from the game's loaded tiles. It stays off in multiplayer and split screen.
- Gave the first person view a picture of its own: a sun and moon that follow the game's clock and season, shadows, clouds, light shafts, rain, wet ground and water that mirror what stands over them, and the game's own characters and vehicles.
- Gave the land in the first person view a shape: hills, valleys round water, level ground under buildings, kerbs along streets and quays over water. It is drawn only. Movement, paths and collisions use the game's flat map as before.
- Made grass stand up in blades near the eye, put trees and bushes in leaf with depth to them, and let grass, earth and sand run raggedly into each other where the game lays them in squares.
- Gave buildings in the first person view walls with thickness, eaves, roofs closed on every side and power lines strung from pole to pole, and gave roofs, walls, floors, furniture, tree trunks and fences the grain of what they are made of.
- Added soft shadows that sharpen where a thing meets the ground, light that bounces between nearby surfaces, cloud shadows, polished floors, and anti-aliasing over time to the first person view. Looking down shows your character's body.
- Added a view from the seat when you are in a vehicle: the dashboard, wheel, doors and roof with the road ahead, seen through glass that carries a little dust and mirrors the sky at a glancing angle.
- Made getting into a vehicle switch to first person, and getting out switch back to the view you had before.
- Made headlights that are on light the road, the verges, characters and the air in front of the vehicle in first person, and made the instruments glow in the cabin when the engine runs at night. Whatever stands in the beam throws its shadow down the road and through the lit air.
- Showed rain falling through headlights as bright streaks, stood it as drops on a vehicle's windows, and let the wipers clear it from the front screen.
- Made night in first person darker: the eye no longer opens up until the ground looks as it does by day.
- Shaded rooms in first person in their corners.
- Lit people and zombies in first person by the sun and sky like the world round them, with smooth skins, soft shadow edges and a rim of sky light, and left them dark at night outside a lamp's reach.
- Gave a vehicle's cabin grained trim and woven seat cloth, and each kind of vehicle a driver's place of its own, with its own wheel, instruments, colours and gear lever: a stitched wheel with a padded hub and switches, stalks behind it, a hooded speedometer and rev counter with a fuel gauge, whose needles follow the vehicle and which light up at night, and a mirror on the screen that shows the road behind. The driver sits in line with the wheel, behind each vehicle's own wheel: higher in a pickup, further forward in a van.
- Made a room at night dim unless something lights it.
- Made the air glow gold towards a low sun in first person, made evening air hazier, and lit leaves seen against the light through.
- Gave people near the eye in first person skins at four times the game's resolution, where finer copies of the game's textures have been made on the player's own machine.
- Gave trees in first person leaves you can make out, let the sun come through their crowns in patches, and made a wood light enough to see in.
- Sharpened everything the game paints up close in first person, and made grass finer and an even green.
- Gave a vehicle's windows straight frames from the driver's seat, and stopped its road wheels showing inside the cabin.
- Trimmed a vehicle's cabin by what each part of it is: the seats in their own cloth, ribbed, pleated, plaid or striped by the kind of vehicle, the dashboard in its own colour with air vents, a radio, a glove box and a band or a whole face of wood where the vehicle has them, and the doors with a cloth panel, an armrest, a handle and a window winder. A dashboard no longer takes the seats' cloth, and the pillars keep the body's paint.
- Gave cars with a lever on the floor its boot and a console between the seats, a van its engine cover there, and a step van the post up the middle of its screen. A lever on the column is on the side towards the middle of the vehicle.
- Made the steering wheel turn with the steering, and the warning lamps under a strip speedometer light for a running engine, headlights and low fuel.
- Gave the driver's door a mirror outside the glass that shows the road behind on that side, and made the mirror on the screen show the road through the vehicle's own back window.
- Showed the bonnet through the windscreen, in the vehicle's paint, with the sky and the sun in its lacquer.
- Kept the driver's place fixed to the vehicle when the head leans.
- Drew the viewer's own vehicle, body and held weapon with a depth of their own again, so their parts hide one another in the right order and are lit by the way they face.
- Rounded people and zombies near the eye in first person where the game's models are faceted.
- Gave a clear day in first person clearer air, fuller colour and more contrast, warm in the sun and cool in the shade.
- Drew vehicles in first person at the size they are in the game's own view. They were drawn half as big again.
- Made beds, wardrobes, sofas, dining chairs, square tables and bedside drawers in first person solid furniture with rounded edges, cushions, pillows and a turned-down blanket, in the colours the game gives each piece.
- Showed a building's siding on the outside of its outside walls in first person and the room's walls on the inside, on every side of the building. The back of a house showed wallpaper outside, and the front showed siding indoors.
- Gave a building in first person one finish on all its outside walls, lapped boards in the colour of its siding, and painted each room in its own colour on every wall, with no wallpaper showing outside or through from the next room. Pipes, cables and vents along a wall run on from one square to the next without a step. Wall tops, door tops and the edges of door and window openings are straight lines, and the openings have sides. A roof's tiles lie on one plane to each slope, slopes meet at the ridge, eaves run along the foot of a roof only, and a slope seen from behind is the colour of the roof.
- Made the left mouse button attack at once in first person, with a firearm or a melee weapon. The game's own view still takes an attack only while the aim is held.
- Made aiming a firearm with the right mouse button narrow the view in first person, easing in and out. Head tracking and the mouse move the picture as far through the sights as at the hip.

- Turned first person off, with the reason in `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.log`, when a frame of it cannot be built or drawn. The fault used to come again every frame and took the game's own panels with it.
- Drew a zombie or survivor whose clothes are still being made from the last whole set of them, as the game does, and left out one the game is not drawing yet.
- Took the grain out of thin cloud and out of the lit air under a low sun.
- Gave back the graphics memory of the first person picture and its four shadow maps when you switch to the game's own view. It is taken again when you switch back.
- Made every part of a frame agree on whether you are in a vehicle, on the frame you get in or out.
- Waited for a vehicle's glass texture to load before reading its windows from it. Read too early, the vehicle was kept as having none.
- Left zombies, survivors and vehicles that stand outside the sun's near shadow map out of it, kept the rain's drops on the graphics card, and stopped the height of distant ground being worked out again up to three times for each piece of the map built.
- Stopped the view jumping to the opposite side when the tracker reports a head turn of more than 90 degrees.
- Gave back the graphics memory of the first person picture when the window changes size. Each size the window had been was held until the game closed.
- Wrote the reason to `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.log` when the mod cannot start, for instance when `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.dll` is not beside the jar. The log used to end without one.
- Put the game's own drawing state back when the first person picture fails part way through a frame.
