# Changelog

## [0.0.0] - 2026-10-07

### Added

- A first person view for Project Zomboid, which has none of its own. `Delete` flies the camera from the game's isometric view to your character's eyes, and back again. In first person the mouse turns the view and the movement keys follow it, on foot and from a seat in a vehicle.
- Head tracking in the first person view, from any tracker that sends the OpenTrack UDP protocol to port `4242`. Turning your head turns the view and leaning moves it, while the mouse keeps the aim: a shot goes where the mouse points, wherever you are looking.
- The reticle follows the shot. While you hold the aim, the game's own reticle is drawn where the shot will land in the view your head has turned. With a weapon in hand and the aim not held, where the game draws none, the mod draws a small crosshair at that point.
- A lean stops at walls, so the view does not end up inside one.
- Sound is heard the way the view faces in first person. What is to the left in the picture is heard on the left, and it turns as the mouse or your head turns the view, where the game's own view hears everything by its place on the screen. As the camera flies between the two views, what you hear turns with it. In first person the game's sounds are also given a camera zoomed all the way in, whatever zoom the game's own view was left at.
- Your character's head turns with yours, up to 80 degrees to either side, in the game's isometric view as well as in first person. In `High`, a window or a wall mirror you stand square in front of in first person shows your character with their head turned as yours is.
- `End` / `Ctrl+Shift+Y` turns head tracking on and off for the session. `Page Up` / `Ctrl+Shift+G` cycles rotation and position, rotation only, and position only. `Page Down` / `Ctrl+Shift+H` switches head yaw between turning about straight up and about the view's own up. From a vehicle's seat the view tips and rolls with the vehicle, on its springs and in a crash, and a lean goes with it. Head yaw there always turns about the view's own up, and the key does nothing until you get out. The tracking mode and the yaw mode are saved when you change them.
- Three ways of aiming down sights, cycled on `Insert` / `Ctrl+Shift+U`, named on the screen and saved. Sights locked, the default, moves your arms and weapon with a lean across the aim, so your eye stays on the sights and the round leaves from where your eye is. Free look leaves the weapon where it is and your head free to move round it, with the game's reticle marking where the round will land. Stock sights eases your head's turn and lean out while the sights are up, apart from tilting.
- Aiming a firearm narrows the view, and head movement is scaled to the zoom, so the narrowed view does not magnify it.
- First person controls while the mouse turns the view: left mouse attacks at once, right mouse aims, `Left Shift` sprints, `Left Alt` jogs and `Left Ctrl` crouches beside your own crouch key. Your saved key bindings are not changed, and the game's own view keeps them as they are.
- A pointer when you need one. The inventory key opens the inventory and loot panels and brings the pointer out, a window or menu of the game's brings it out for as long as it is open, and holding the middle mouse button brings it out while held. Head tracking carries on while the pointer is out.
- `E` acts on what the middle of the view is on: a door, a window, a curtain, a light switch, a container or something lying on the ground.
- A held weapon is drawn before the eye as a first person game shows it: low and to the right at the hip, raised with the sights, trailing a turn of the view, swaying with each step and kicking with each shot, with a flash at the muzzle. Where the shot goes and what a swing hits are the game's own.
- A melee attack is drawn in time with the game's own. The weapon is drawn back, comes round and lands on the frame the game lands the hit. What it lands on stops it and it springs back, and a miss goes on through. A swing, an overhead blow, an uppercut, a blow onto the ground, the thrust of a knife or a spear and a shove each have their own movement, and your shoulders turn with them.
- A quick click attacks with a melee weapon as it does with a firearm: one the game saw held for a single frame is enough.
- The first person view goes on past the map the game has loaded. At 1920 by 1080 the game keeps about 75 tiles of map round you, and the view ended there in haze: from a rooftop twenty floors up there was little to see but the roof. Beyond it the land is now drawn for 768 tiles each way from the picture of its map the game ships, with a plain block for every building as high as its floors. Nothing else is drawn out there: no trees, vehicles, furniture or zombies.
- In first person a zombie is hidden by what stands between it and your eye, and by nothing else. The game fades a zombie in as your character comes to see the square it stands on: in first person that was a zombie appearing out of nothing a pace away as you came round a corner, and none at all past about thirty tiles in the open. Every zombie in front of the view is now drawn whole for as far as the view goes, on foot as from a vehicle. On a server where players can hurt each other, zombies are shown as the game shows them to everyone.
- In first person a room within 40 tiles is whole before you can see into it. The game shows a room once your character has had it in sight, which it works out a moment after they get there, and built by that a bathroom was empty when its door opened and its sink and toilet appeared with you standing in it. What is in a room is now built whether or not the game has shown it, and the room's own walls, doors and curtains hide it until a door opens, you turn a corner, look in at a window or lean round a frame. Such a room is lit as the rooms in sight are until the game has a light for it. The far end of a street is also built before you turn to face it. On a server where players can fight, a room is still filled only as the game shows it to everyone.
- Lines painted on roads are drawn as paint. The game lays a road's centre lines, edge lines, stop bars, crossings, arrows and parking bays as small pictures, one a square, and seen from the ground each had a saw edge and stepped sideways at every square. First person now draws each as the shape it is, in the game's colour: straight edges and one width along a road, sharp from close by and clean far down it. It is so in every graphics mode, and `OwnTextures=false` puts the game's pictures back.
- Pavements, car parks, forecourts and yards are laid, not bulged. A pavement along a shop front lies level at the shop's footing out to the far kerb, the road as high at one kerb as at the other, and past the end of the building comes back to the land as a gentle ramp, where on a hillside the square of paving against each wall climbed or dropped as much as a whole storey to the door. A row of shops with narrow passages between stands on one level, and a town's streets lose the swells of open country. The strip of concrete between a road and its pavement is drawn as paint is, of one width along the street with a clean edge.
- Three graphics modes, cycled on `F9`, named on the screen and saved as `Quality`. `Low`, which the mod starts in, leaves the game's isometric view as the game draws it and draws first person from the game's own sprites, models and textures, at a little over three fifths of the pixels each way and seeing 48 tiles. `Medium` and `High` draw both views with what the mod made, `High` in full and `Medium` at four fifths of the pixels each way, seeing 64 tiles instead of 80, with the game's own plants and without the shading of corners, the lit air, the reflections and the depth in near surfaces. `Quality=Automatic`, set in the file, begins as `High` and draws less each time the picture falls under 45 frames a second for three seconds.
- In `Medium` and `High` the isometric view opens a building as the game does. The floors over yours are left out of the buildings the game leaves them out of, your own and one that stands between the camera and you, the walls the game cuts away are cut down to a low course, and a room is as light as the game has it: dark until your character has seen into it, and dimmer while they cannot see it.
- In `Medium` and `High`, content the mod made in place of the game's own: models of the pump action shotgun, the hunting rifle and rifle scopes, arms and hands that close on a held weapon and work its bolt, slide, magazine and loading port, furniture, a moving picture on a television that is switched on, bars with nothing coming in and with a channel or a tape the show that is on, cut from shot to shot, pictures on walls, some of the lettered signs on shops and other walls with the wording the game's own carry, the flag hung on a wall, plants, surface textures, built windows, doors, curtains and handrails, skins for zombies and for the living, and what they wear: the game's own picture of each garment, head of hair and beard enlarged with its outline smoothed and the weave, knit, grain or strands of what it is made of laid over it, and T-shirt prints, blood and dirt of the mod's own. Each is a row of `[Content]` in `CameraUnlock.ini`, ten in all, `true` unless you change it. Set a row to `false` and the game's own is drawn there, as the game or another mod has it.
- In `Medium` and `High`, the game's x2, x4 and x8 scopes are drawn as a scope you look through. Its eyepiece shows the world at the scope's power with the reticle in it, and the picture is whole only with your eye behind the scope: off its axis or too far back, a shadow closes in. Lean in to the eyepiece and the picture grows with it.
- Snow that has settled is a surface of its own in the first person view, and in the isometric view in `Medium` and `High`. It lies in swells, lumps and wind ripples, ends in a rounded lip where the ground shows through, is dimmer and bluer in its hollows and glitters in the sun, and less of it lies under a tree or the eaves of a house. It keeps your latest footprints and the ruts of the vehicle you drive until the snow is gone.
- Trees, bushes and other plants carry the snow that lies, as much as the game says lies: on the upper side of a crown's sprigs and its boughs, as caps and clumps on a bush or a plant drawn from the game's own picture, along the top of a limb and up the weather side of a bole, and caught in the ivy. With a little snow lying they have a dusting, and with deep snow they are heavy with it. The snow on a plant is lit as the snow on the ground beside it is, and a bough laden with it swings less in the wind. Grass stands in the snow as deep as the snow lies at its root, bent over by the snow on it, and stands clear of it under a tree or the eaves where the ground shows.
- Three picture styles, cycled on `F8`, named on the screen and saved as `Style`: `Vivid`, the default, `Natural` and `Bleak`. A style changes how the mod's picture is developed from the light in it and nothing else.
- Two players at one screen each have a first person view of their own. `Delete` tapped switches the first player's view, tapped twice the second player's, and held for 0.4 seconds both players'. The second player's head tracker is listened for on UDP port `4243` (`SecondPlayerUdpPort`), the first player's on `4242` as before. On a controller in first person the aiming stick turns the view, the right trigger attacks, and with a firearm in hand the left trigger raises the sights. The tracking, yaw, aim and graphics modes and the picture style are shared by both players. Each player's character turns its head with its own player's. A third and fourth player keep the game's own view.
- Two players at one screen can be one above the other. The game puts them side by side, each with half the width. `F7` goes between that and player 1 above player 2, each with the whole width and half the height, names on the screen which it went to and saves it as `SplitScreen` (`Vertical` or `Horizontal`), which is read again while the game runs. With one, three or four players the screen is as the game lays it out and the key does nothing.
- `FieldOfView` in `CameraUnlock.ini` sets the first person field of view, in degrees from the bottom of the picture to the top: `65` by default, from `40` to `110`. It is read again while the game runs, as are `Quality`, `Style`, `FrameBlending` and the `[Content]` rows, so a change shows without a restart.
- A flashlight you carry follows your head and not your aim. `LightMultiplier` sets how far it turns for each degree your head turns.
- A flashlight you carry throws a beam in the mod's view: a bright middle with a dim spill round it, which stops at whatever stands in it and lays that thing's shadow on what is behind. It lights walls, floors, ceilings, furniture, zombies, other people, rain and snow alike, and reaches up a staircase to the floor above before you are on it. The game's own way of lighting a flashlight, every floor tile in a wide fan on the level you stand on, is left out of the picture and still decides what your character can see and who can see them.
- Multiplayer. The first person view and head tracking work when you join a server, and only you need the mod: the server and the other players do not. Other players' names and speech are written over their heads in the first person view. Your character's head turns with yours on your own screen only.
- Fair play where players can hurt each other, which is any server with PvP on. There a lean does not move a shot: with the sights up the lean across the aim eases out and the round leaves from your character. Walking sideways or backwards puts your character in the game's own aiming stance, so they face the way the view does.
- Settings are in `CameraUnlock.ini` in the game folder, which the mod creates when it starts and finds none. A setting left at `default` follows `Defaults.ini`, shared with other head tracking mods that keep their settings in a `CameraUnlock.ini`. Every key above is a row of `[Hotkeys]` and can be moved to other keys or left empty. A key pressed in the game's main menu, or while you are typing in one of its text boxes, does nothing.
- The mod's settings in the game's own options. The `Mods` tab has a `Head Tracking` section with the tracking mode, the aim mode, the yaw mode, the field of view, the graphics mode, the picture style, frame blending, how two players split the screen and each row of `[Content]`. What you apply there is saved to `CameraUnlock.ini` and takes effect at once, and the section shows the current settings each time the options open.
- An installer with no mod loader. `install.cmd` copies `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.jar` and `CameraUnlockCore.dll` next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe` and writes `ProjectZomboid64.site.json` from the game's own launch settings, so the game's Java runtime loads the mod. It finds a Steam copy of the game by itself and takes any other copy as a path. `uninstall.cmd` leaves `CameraUnlock.ini` in place.
- Each save starts in the game's own view. Head tracking moves the view while the game is not paused and your character is alive.
- The mod follows one tracker at a time and listens on IPv4. If a second program sends to the port, the mod stays with the first until that one has been silent for two seconds, and the log names both.
- If a game update changes something the mod uses, the mod stays out and the game runs without it. `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.log`, next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe`, names what changed.
