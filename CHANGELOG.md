# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Made kerbs in the first person view go round corners and bends. A street that turns or curves is drawn on the game's map as a stair of squares, and the kerb followed it step by step. Now the kerb runs as the street's edge would: straight where the squares run straight, at a slant where they step evenly, and round a corner in a curve. The ground on both sides is cut along the same line, and what stands or grows beside it keeps to its own side. A kerb along the wall of a building still turns the building's corner square.
- Hung pictures of the mod's own on walls in the first person view. A framed photograph, a painting or a poster was the game's sprite of it, some thirty pixels across, stretched over a frame a pace from your eye. Now family photographs, the paintings of houses and the gallery, sports posters, the posters of bands and singers, the film posters outside the cinema, cork boards, whiteboards and the doctors' certificates each show a picture made for the mod at many times the detail. A poster that hangs along several squares is one picture across all of them. Shop signs and the game's own view are unchanged.
- Made the game's rifle scopes (x2, x4 and x8) work in the first person view. A scope is drawn as a scope of the mod's own, and its eyepiece shows the world a second time, from the scope's front lens along its axis, at the scope's power counted from the unaided view: through the x8 a thing is eight times as large as with nothing aimed. The picture is whole only with your eye on the scope's axis at the right distance behind it. Sights locked keeps your eye there. In the free look modes you bring your head to the scope, and off its axis the picture dims, a shadow comes in and the eyepiece goes dark. The reticle stays on the same point of the world wherever your eye is. While the sights are up the mouse turns the aim by one over the scope's power. Leaning in behind a scope stops where its shadow has closed half the picture.
- Rebuilt doors, curtains and glazed walls in the first person view, which were the game's small sprites stretched over a board. A door is a leaf as thick as a door in its sprite's colour, with glass in its lights, mouldings round its panels or boards on ledges, and a knob on each face. A curtain is cloth hung in folds from a rod, drawn across or pulled back as the game has it. A shop's front or the side of a sun room is glass in a frame, bar by bar. A window with an arched head is built too, its frame following the arch.
- Gave surfaces in the first person view depth. A boarded wall, a plank fence, a shingled roof, a wooden floor, a gravel drive, bare earth and tarmac were flat sheets with a picture on them, and looked it as soon as you moved your head. Within about eight paces each now has the height of what it shows: the joints between boards are grooves, stones stand up out of a drive and clods out of earth, tarmac is pitted. Lean and the near edge of a stone hides the foot of the one behind it, a low sun throws each stone and each board edge a shadow of its own, and hollows are darker than what stands over them.
- Regrew the grass in the first person view. It was flat upright cards with straight blades cut out of them. Now every lawn and every patch of long grass is grown plant by plant: tufts of fine turf whose blades bend and fan out from one root, coarse broad-bladed grass, tussocks that arch over, dead straw, clover, and stalks gone to seed with a spike or a nodding head on top. Which of them grows where changes from one yard to the next. The wind crosses it in gusts, with the grass lying over under each and standing again behind it, and the grass parts round your feet when you stand in it.
- Gave trees in the first person view bark of their own. Every trunk had the same picture of bark, pulled out of shape: squeezed round a thin trunk and stretched up a thick one. Now there are eight barks, oak, linden, maple, birch, pine, hemlock, holly and dogwood, and a tree has the bark of its species, or one of two or three where the game's species has none of its own. The bark is the same size round a trunk as up it and near enough the same size on a sapling as on an old tree. No two trunks show the same piece of it: each starts somewhere else on its picture and round its trunk, half have it the other way up, and each is a little lighter or darker than its neighbour. Up a tall trunk the picture no longer comes round again every half pace.
- Stopped the grain of fences, boarded walls, wooden floors and shingled roofs in the first person view from repeating every pace or two. The same knot used to come round all the way down a fence. Each run of boards and each band of shingles is now slid along its length by its own amount, and half are turned end for end.
- Made a window show your own character in its glass in first person, turned head and all, with their eyes on your own eye line. Stand square in front of a shut, unbroken window on the floor you are on: the reflection is faint by day, when the street outside outshines it, and plain at night. A mirror hung on a wall shows you too, and there your character covers what is behind them as they would in a real mirror.
- Gave the pump action shotgun (`Base.Shotgun`) a model of the mod's own in first person: a walnut stock and fore-end with cut checkering, a blued receiver and barrel with a ventilated rib and a brass bead, worn pale along its edges. It is lit as steel and varnished wood are, by the sun, the sky and the room you stand in. After a shot the fore-end slides back and forward with the hand that works it, the bolt opens with it, and the spent shell is thrown out of the port and falls away. The hand on the fore-end keeps one hold on it through the stroke. Where the shot goes is unchanged. The sawn-off shotgun and every other weapon are drawn with the game's own models as before, and a scope, recoil pad or choke fitted to the shotgun is not drawn on the new model.
- Rebuilt windows in the first person view. A window was the game's small sprite stretched over the wall, so its frame and bars were blocks the size of a thumb and its glass a speckle. Now each one is built: a frame through the thickness of the wall, a sill on the outside, every bar where the game's sprite has it, and a sheet of glass in each light that mirrors the sky, carries a little dust and glows when a lamp is lit behind it at night. An open window shows its raised sash and a broken one the shards left round its edge. Arched and round windows, church glass, shop fronts and the glass in doors are still drawn from the game's sprites.
- Rebuilt the crowns of trees and bushes in the first person view out of leaves. Each crown is now a few hundred sprigs of leaves, linden, maple, birch, pine, hemlock or holly by the tree's species, standing where the game paints the tree's crown and in the colour the game has it at that time of year. They lie along the crown's surface with a shaded layer of them inside, so a crown is full from every side. A tree is leaves in front of leaves against the sky, lit by the sun as a round mass, where it was five painted cards crossing at its trunk. Trees further than about 40 tiles keep the cards.
- Kept the first person view's surface textures sharp when seen at a slant, which is how the ground is seen from standing height.
- Made your character's head follow yours. In the game's own view your character turns, nods and tilts their head as you do, up to 80 degrees to either side, and that is all head tracking does there. In first person the same turn shows in your character's shadow. Single player only, like the first person view.
- Made `F8` fly the camera between the game's own view and the first person view, in about a second each way. Going in, the first person picture of the same scene fades in over the game's, then the camera turns to face the way your character does and comes down to their eyes. Going out is the same in reverse. The mouse and the movement keys change over when the camera lands. Getting into and out of a vehicle uses the same flight.
- Made the log record an error the game catches from the mod, once for each fault. It went only to the game's own `console.txt` before. Wrote the first person frame time to the log once a minute, down from every five seconds, so a long session leaves a log the other lines can be found in.
- Made the mod stay out of the game after a game update that renames or removes something it uses, so the game runs without it. The log names what changed and ends with `the mod stays out of the game:`.
- Added `FieldOfView` to `CameraUnlock.ini`: the first person view's field of view in degrees from the bottom of the picture to the top, `65` by default and `40` to `110`. The game has no field of view setting of its own. The mod reads it again while the game runs, so a change shows within a second or so of saving the file, and the reticle, the weapon in your hands and head tracking all follow it. Aiming a firearm still narrows the view by the same ratio.
- Added a settings file, `CameraUnlock.ini`, which the mod creates next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe` the first time it starts. It holds the tracker's UDP port, the two smoothing values, the lean limits, the wall check, the carried light's multiplier and the hotkeys, which can now be rebound. A setting left on `default` takes its value from `%AppData%\CameraUnlock\Defaults.ini`, which every head tracking mod that keeps its settings in `CameraUnlock.ini` reads.
- Made the mod try the tracker's UDP port again every half second if it cannot be opened when the game starts, for instance because another game with a head tracking mod is still running. It starts listening when the port is free, with no restart. The log records the reason the port could not be opened.
- Saved the tracking mode (`Page Up`) and the yaw mode (`Page Down`) when changed, as the aim mode already was. `End` still lasts only until the game closes.
- Made the installer put `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.dll` next to the jar. It reads and writes the settings file.
- Added four ways of aiming down a firearm's sights with head tracking on, cycled on `Insert` / `Ctrl+Shift+U` and saved when changed: sights locked (the default), free look with a marker, true free look and stock sights. In sights locked a lean across the aim moves the weapon and where the shot starts with your eye, so the sights stay lined up and the shot lands where they point. In the free look modes the weapon stays put and your head moves round it. In stock sights your head's turn and lean ease out while the sights are up and only its tilt stays.
- Kept the weapon in your hands on your aim when you turn or lean your head, and pointed its barrel at the place the shot will land at the hip.
- Kept the left hand on a firearm's fore-end. It used to follow the game's own animation, and flew off the weapon, dragged its sleeve across the view or vanished when you turned or walked.
- Joined the hands on a held weapon to their sleeves with a wrist that bends as a wrist does, at the hip, with the sights up and through a swing. Each hand is turned on the grip to meet the arm behind it, the forearm comes up to the grip from below, and the cuff is the shape of the wrist and closes over it.
- Let leaning in reach the full 0.40 m at the hip, and stopped it just short of the rear sight with the sights up.
- Held a weapon before the eye in first person. A firearm sits low and to the right at the hip and comes up to the middle of the picture when the aim is held; a melee weapon is held up at the lower right and its swing is seen from the eye. The arms that hold it come with it. The weapon trails a turn of the view, sways with each step, drops while sprinting and dips on a crouch or a landing.
- Made a shot in first person kick the weapon back and up, throw a flash from its muzzle and light the people and zombies near it for the length of the flash. Where the shot goes and what it hits are unchanged.
- Drew a small crosshair where the shot would land in first person, with a weapon in hand and the aim not held. The game's own reticle takes over while the aim is held, as before.
- Added a pointer to the first person view. The inventory key shows the inventory and loot panels and hands the mouse back as a pointer, and the same key or `Escape` puts them away and returns to the look. The pointer also comes out by itself while a window, dialog or menu of the game's is open, and for as long as the middle mouse button is held. The picture, head tracking and the game carry on meanwhile.
- Added a first person view. `F8` switches between it and the game's own view: the mouse turns the camera, the movement keys follow it, and the world is drawn from the game's loaded tiles. It stays off in multiplayer and split screen.

- Gave the land in the first person view a shape: hills, valleys round water, level ground under buildings, kerbs along streets and quays over water. It is drawn only. Movement, paths and collisions use the game's flat map as before.
- Made grass stand up in blades near the eye, put trees and bushes in leaf with depth to them, and let grass, earth and sand run raggedly into each other where the game lays them in squares.

### Fixed
- Stopped the back and the west side of a building in first person from being drawn in the wrong material. A house that is part brick and part boards, a brick block with a boarded garage or porch on it, say, had every wall on its north in whatever most of its south front showed and every wall on its west in whatever most of its east side showed, so the back of the wooden garage came out brick, or the side of the brick block boards downstairs and brick upstairs. Each part of a building now shows on its north and west what it shows on its south and east.
- Stopped ceilings, and walls looked along, from flickering light and dark in the first person view, and a wall the sun only grazes from flickering in patches. The pass that shades corners and crevices took such a surface to face the other way on some frames.
- Stopped a flat roof's covering from flickering against the floor it lies on in the first person view.
- Fixed the game running out of memory on a long trip in first person. The mod kept every building it had drawn, and with each one all of its squares after the game had unloaded them. Crossing a few towns filled the game's 3 GB.
- Turned first person off, with the reason in `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.log`, when a frame of it cannot be built or drawn. The fault used to come again every frame and took the game's own panels with it.
- Drew a zombie or survivor whose clothes are still being made from the last whole set of them, as the game does, and left out one the game is not drawing yet.
- Took the grain out of thin cloud and out of the lit air under a low sun.
- Gave back the graphics memory of the first person picture and its four shadow maps when you switch to the game's own view. It is taken again when you switch back.

- Waited for a vehicle's glass texture to load before reading its windows from it. Read too early, the vehicle was kept as having none.
- Left zombies, survivors and vehicles that stand outside the sun's near shadow map out of it, kept the rain's drops on the graphics card, and stopped the height of distant ground being worked out again up to three times for each piece of the map built.
- Stopped the view jumping to the opposite side when the tracker reports a head turn of more than 90 degrees.
