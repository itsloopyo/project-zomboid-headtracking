# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Gave your character and the other living skins of the mod's own in first person, in place of the game's painted ones: a lifelike face and skin with its grain for each sex and each of the game's five skin tones, and for a man the hair on chest, arms and legs where his character has it. Hair, beards and clothes are the game's, worn over it. A new row of `[Content]`, `OwnPeople`, switches them off, like the others while the game runs.
- Made `F9` the graphics mode key, in either view. It goes from `Low` to `Medium` to `High` and round again, says on the screen which mode it went to, and saves it as `Quality` in `CameraUnlock.ini` so the game starts in it next time. `Low` is now as near the game's own look as there is: the game's own view is the game's own picture, and first person is drawn with the game's own sprites, models and textures in place of everything the mod made. `Medium` and `High` draw both views with everything the mod made. `F9` used to switch the mod's picture of the game's own view on and off, and only there. `F8` is as it was: it switches between the game's own view and first person. `Automatic` is still what the row starts at and can only be set in the file.
- Gave the leaves of trees and bushes in the first person view light of their own. Each leaf of a sprig is turned its own way and domed, so the sun is full on one leaf and glancing on the next, the waxed upper side of a leaf throws the sun off as a glint, and a leaf with the sun behind it glows: looking up through a crown at midday, the leaves overhead are lit from above. A crown is several boughs with hollows between them, bright on each bough's top and shaded in the hollows, where it was one evenly lit ball. The pale line round every leaf, which showed against a dark wood, is gone. A tree stood under holds together: its trunk runs on up the middle of the crown and puts out limbs and branches to the boughs, the nearest sprigs are on twigs, the crown has more leaves inside it, and leaves are cut away only within a hand's reach of the eye instead of an arm's. The dark brown leaves that went up a trunk in a spiral under the crown are gone: they were grown on the shade the game paints on the trunk there.
- Outlined what `E` would act on in first person. The game's own highlight of the thing under the pointer is drawn in its own view, beneath the first person picture, and nothing showed which door, window, curtain, light switch, cupboard, body or dropped item the key would go to. Whatever the middle of the view is on, within reach, now has a pale line round it and is a little lighter.
- Added `Quality` to `CameraUnlock.ini`, for a graphics card that cannot keep up with the whole first person picture: `High`, `Medium`, `Low` or `Automatic`. `High` is the picture as it was. `Medium` draws four fifths of the pixels each way, sees 64 tiles instead of 80, uses shadow maps of half the size and draws the far shadows and what is overhead on alternate frames. `Low` draws three fifths of the pixels each way, sees 48 tiles and leaves out the shading of corners, the lit air and the depth in near surfaces. On the PC the mod is made on, the graphics card's work for a frame goes from 7.2 ms at `High` to 5.2 at `Medium` and 3.8 at `Low`. The row starts at `Automatic`, which begins at `High` and goes down a step each time the first person view has been drawn fewer than 45 times a second for three seconds, and stays there until the game is closed. The row is read again while the game runs.
- Gave zombies near the eye in the first person view skins of the mod's own in place of the game's painted ones: a lifelike face for each sex and skin tone, further gone in each of the game's four states of decay, on skin with pores, settled blood, veins, rot and open wounds, eight times the game's size each way. A new row of `[Content]` in `CameraUnlock.ini`, `OwnZombies`, switches them: set it to `false` and zombies wear the game's own skins again, a second or so after the file is saved. Clothes, hair and the bodies of the living are the game's as before.
- Made everything the mod made itself optional. `CameraUnlock.ini` has a new `[Content]` section with six rows, all `true` to start with: `OwnWeapons` (the mod's shotgun, rifle and scope models), `OwnFurniture`, `OwnPictures`, `OwnPlants`, `OwnTextures` and `OwnBuildings` (built windows, doors and curtains, and the finish on the outside of a wall). With a row set to `false` the first person view draws the game's own there, so a weapon, a piece of furniture or a texture that another mod changes shows as that mod has it. The rows are read again while the game runs, like `FieldOfView`: save the file and the view changes within a second or so.
- Laid tiled floors in the first person view tile by tile: each tile a face of its own in the colour the game paints it, with a straight edge and a joint between it and the next, where the game's sixty dots of floor were magnified into soft squares. A fitted carpet is one colour all over, without the blots the game's scattered dots made of it. Floors of brick laid in bond and paving of odd stones are still the game's sprites.

A paper with flowers on it is printed sprig by sprig. This is so far on the two walls of a room that the game draws from inside it, the north and the west.
- Gave the hunting rifle (`Base.HuntingRifle`) a model of the mod's own in first person: a bolt action sporting rifle with a walnut stock checkered at the grip and the fore-end, a dark fore-end tip, a blued barrel and receiver, and open sights. After a shot its bolt is worked: the handle turns up, the bolt comes back, the fired case is thrown out to the right, and the bolt shuts.

The ground on both sides is cut along the same line, and what stands or grows beside it keeps to its own side. A kerb along the wall of a building still turns the building's corner square.

A poster that hangs along several squares is one picture across all of them. Shop signs and the game's own view are unchanged.

The picture is whole only with your eye on the scope's axis at the right distance behind it. Sights locked keeps your eye there. In the free look modes you bring your head to the scope, and off its axis the picture dims, a shadow comes in and the eyepiece goes dark. The reticle stays on the same point of the world wherever your eye is. While the sights are up the mouse turns the aim by one over the scope's power. Leaning in behind a scope stops where its shadow has closed half the picture.
- Rebuilt doors, curtains and glazed walls in the first person view, which were the game's small sprites stretched over a board. A curtain is cloth hung in folds from a rod, drawn across or pulled back as the game has it. A shop's front or the side of a sun room is glass in a frame, bar by bar. A window with an arched head is built too, its frame following the arch.

A boarded wall, a plank fence, a shingled roof, a wooden floor, a gravel drive, bare earth and tarmac were flat sheets with a picture on them, and looked it as soon as you moved your head. Lean and the near edge of a stone hides the foot of the one behind it, a low sun throws each stone and each board edge a shadow of its own, and hollows are darker than what stands over them.

### Fixed
- Drew the dead as bodies in the first person view. A body on the ground was a dark rectangle the size of a doormat. The game keeps a picture of each body, clothes, blood and all, and that picture now lies on the ground where the body does. A body the game's own view has not yet had in it has no picture, and shows once it has.
- Stopped glasses, hats and whatever else a character wears on their head from showing through the head in first person. The game's shader takes the depth of such a piece from the body it hangs on, which still carried the shift the game fits a whole person among its sprites with, so the piece was drawn nearer the eye than the head it was on.
- Left the game's own rain, puddles, splashes and fog undrawn while the first person picture covers the game's view. The game built its rain drop by drop on its logic thread and wrote it out corner by corner on its render thread, to be painted over by the picture's own rain. In rain the first person view now takes a seventh less of the logic thread and a fifth less of the render thread. The weather itself, and what is wet, are as they were.
- Made the game itself about a quarter faster among a crowd of zombies, in its own view as in first person. The game kept working out three answers that do not change: for every one of the dozens of times a frame it wants a zombie's state machine it looked it up in a map of what the zombie is made of, after climbing a chain of classes to find what kind of thing a state machine is, and for every bone of every character on every frame it asked whether the bone hangs on another skeleton by walking a list that is nearly always empty. Each is now found once and kept. Nothing the game does comes out differently. With 300 zombies standing on the screen the game's own view went from about 100 to about 126 frames a second on the PC the mod is made on (108 and 95 without, 127, 125 and 126 with), and its logic thread from 9.7 ms a frame to 7.6.
- Stopped head tracking from costing the first person view most of its frame rate. A head on a tracker is never where it was to the last bit, and the view rebuilt the world round the eye on every frame for it: 78 frames a second with a head swaying a centimetre, where a head held still got 193. A chunk is now rebuilt once the eye has moved 6 mm from where it was built from, or turned a quarter of a degree, and the same sway runs at 197.
- Stopped a crowd from bringing the first person view to a crawl. With 300 zombies in front of you it drew 17 frames a second, most of each frame spent reading through the names of textures for skins there was no room left to make. That is gone, the game no longer draws each of them into its own view under the picture that covers it, and one who stands inside the near shadow map is not cast into the far one as well: the same crowd is now drawn 69 times a second, where the game's own view draws it 126 times.
- Drew the first person view faster with the picture unchanged: 188 to 242 frames a second across the test scenes on the PC the mod is made on, from 162 to 191. The solid world is drawn for its depth first and lit only where it is the nearest thing, a chunk waits out the time its interval stood for on a PC that draws faster than a hundred frames a second, and the near chunks of a still view are read through thirty times a second and not on every frame.
- Stopped walking from costing the first person view a third of its frame rate. The view rebuilt the seven chunks of the world round the eye on every frame the eye moved, however little, because the flat sprites in them are turned to face the eye. A chunk is now kept until the eye has gone two degrees round its nearest edge, which turns a sprite by less than can be seen, so on foot only the chunk you stand in is rebuilt every frame. On the PC the mod is made on a walk through a garden went from 97 to 161 frames a second and a run from 150 to 166. Driving at speed is as it was: at 110 km/h the eye passes that angle every frame.
- Stopped the driver's seat from building and drawing the whole world behind the car. With a rear view mirror in the cab the first person view kept every chunk in sight range all the way round, where on foot it keeps what is in front. It now keeps what is in front and the narrow strip the mirror shows, about a third fewer chunks on a highway, and the ones ahead are built sooner.
- Made first person about a third faster on the move. The view rebuilt each piece of the world round you whenever you moved, because flat sprites were turned to face you and every tree, bush, rock and tuft of long grass was made by its own distance from your eye. Flat sprites are now turned by the graphics card as the picture is drawn, plants take their detail from which of five rings of distance their piece of the map is in, and only the lawn, ferns, flowers and fallen leaves are remade as you go. On the PC the mod is made on, driving at 110 km/h went from 89 to about 115 frames a second, driving at 40 km/h from 98 to 132, a walk from 161 to 187 and a run from 166 to 197, the last three now as fast as standing still. A tree's crown and limbs change a step at a time as you come up to it, where they changed by degrees, and a round trunk is made out to 40 tiles, where it was 60. The world takes a quarter more graphics memory.

The pictures are now read on other cores from the moment the save is being played and handed to the graphics card a few a frame, the world is built once unseen in the first second or two, and a frame builds the far parts of the world for five milliseconds at most and leaves the rest to the next frames. The longest frame of that switch is now about 60 ms on the same PC. The same limit holds after a jump across the map and as the head turns fast: the far end of the view fills in over a few frames.
