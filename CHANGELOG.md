# Changelog

## [0.0.0] - 2026-10-07

### Added

- A first person view for Project Zomboid, which has none of its own. `Delete` flies the camera from the game's isometric view to your character's eyes, and back again. In first person the mouse turns the view and the movement keys follow it, on foot and from a seat in a vehicle.
- Head tracking in the first person view, from any tracker that sends the OpenTrack UDP protocol to port `4242`. Turning your head turns the view and leaning moves it, while the mouse keeps the aim: a shot goes where the mouse points, wherever you are looking.
- In first person the eye is steady as you walk and run: the bob and sway of each step are taken out of it, and crouching or sitting still lowers it smoothly.
- The reticle follows the shot. While you hold the aim, the game's own reticle is drawn where the shot will land in the view your head has turned. With a weapon in hand and the aim not held, where the game draws none, the mod draws a small crosshair at that point.
- A lean stops at walls, so the view does not end up inside one.
- Sound is heard the way the view faces in first person. What is to the left in the picture is heard on the left, and it turns as the mouse or your head turns the view, where the game's own view hears everything by its place on the screen. As the camera flies between the two views, what you hear turns with it. In first person the game's sounds are also given a camera zoomed all the way in, whatever zoom the game's own view was left at.
- Walls and rooms are heard in first person. A sound behind a wall, a shut door, a shut window or a floor comes through quieter and muffled, less so where it can come round through a doorway or an open window near by. The room you stand in rings: a tiled bathroom or a bare garage for a long time, a furnished bedroom hardly at all, a big hall or warehouse longer than a small room, and open ground not at all. A sound behind you in the same room is no longer muffled, as the game's own view had it. The game's own view sounds as it always has.
- Your character's head turns with yours, up to 80 degrees to either side, in the game's isometric view as well as in first person. In `High`, a window or a wall mirror you stand square in front of in first person shows your character with their head turned as yours is.
- `Page Up` cycles rotation and position, rotation only, and position only. `Page Down` switches head yaw between turning about straight up and about the view's own up. From a vehicle's seat the view tips and rolls with the vehicle, on its springs and in a crash, and a lean goes with it. Head yaw there always turns about the view's own up, and the key does nothing until you get out. The tracking mode and the yaw mode are saved when you change them.
- Three ways of aiming down sights, cycled on `Insert`, named on the screen and saved. Sights locked, the default, moves your arms and weapon with a lean across the aim, so your eye stays on the sights and the round leaves from where your eye is. Free look leaves the weapon where it is and your head free to move round it, with the game's reticle marking where the round will land. Stock sights eases your head's turn and lean out while the sights are up, apart from tilting.
- Aiming a firearm narrows the view, and head movement is scaled to the zoom, so the narrowed view does not magnify it.
- First person controls while the mouse turns the view: left mouse attacks at once, right mouse aims, `Left Shift` sprints, `Left Alt` jogs and `Left Ctrl` crouches beside your own crouch key. Your saved key bindings are not changed, and the game's own view keeps them as they are.
- A pointer when you need one. The inventory key opens the inventory and loot panels and brings the pointer out, a window or menu of the game's brings it out for as long as it is open, and holding the middle mouse button brings it out while held. Head tracking carries on while the pointer is out.
- `E` acts on what the middle of the view is on: a door, a window, a curtain, a light switch, a container or something lying on the ground.
- With the pointer out, a right click opens the game's own menu for what the pointer is on in the first person picture, with everything on that square, as a right click does in the isometric view, so a sink can be drunk from or washed at.
- A held weapon is drawn before the eye as a first person game shows it: low and to the right at the hip, raised with the sights, trailing a turn of the view, swaying with each step and kicking with each shot, with a small flash out ahead of the muzzle for a frame or two, except in the game's own look (`Low`, `Automatic` at `Stock`). A shot throws the weapon up and back into your shoulder, and it springs back within half a second, a pump shotgun hardest and a pistol least, by the game's own recoil figure for each weapon. A sawn-off shotgun has no stock to put to your shoulder, so with the sights up it kicks in your hands as it does at the hip, its muzzle thrown up far more than a stocked gun's. A pistol of the mod's own drives its slide back and forward with each shot and throws the case out to the right; after the last round its slide stays back until a loaded magazine is in and the pistol is racked, and a reload shows the magazine leave the grip and the new one go in. While the game is paused a shot's kick and a shell thrown from the gun hold where they are, and go on from there when you unpause. The shot still goes where you aimed. The flash is the shot's only light: the game's own light of a shot is left out of the view while it shows. `MuzzleFlash=false` in `CameraUnlock.ini`, or `Muzzle flash` in the game's options, leaves the flash out, and a shot lights what is round you as the game's own does. Where the shot goes and what a swing hits are the game's own.
- Zombies are wounded where you shoot them. In first person a firearm hits the part of a zombie the aim is on, and each arm and leg keeps count of the shots it has taken. Once one limb's shots add up to half the health the zombie has left, the limb is wounded: a wounded leg slows the zombie to a shamble, its body lurching over that leg with each step, and a wounded arm sags. At all of the health left the limb is ruined: a ruined leg drops the zombie, and it gets up crawling, and a ruined arm hangs dead from the shoulder. The game takes only a twentieth of a shot in an arm or a leg off a zombie's health, and left to itself makes a crawler only by chance. Wounds are single player only: on a server, your own co-op host included, zombies take shots as the game has them.
- Shot limbs come off. A ruined arm or leg shot again comes off at the elbow or knee, or at the shoulder or hip for a shot high on it, and so does one hit by a shot of twice the zombie's health. The limb flies off, tumbles and comes to rest lying on the ground, where it stays for two minutes. In first person the stump and the cut end show raw meat. Blood sprays on the floor where it comes off and where it lands, and a zombie missing a limb leaves a trail of blood that dries and goes after a few minutes. A body keeps its missing limbs, also after a save is loaded. A shotgun headshot that kills can knock the head off, which flies the same way, or burst it in a spray of blood and meat. A zombie whose leg is ruined, and not shot off, sometimes gets back up and shambles on instead of crawling. Single player only, as wounds are; the flying limbs are drawn in first person only.
- In `Medium` and `High`, blood on floors and walls is drawn as blood in first person. The game's picture of a splat is a few dozen red dots, and up close it was a grid of red squares: it now has a smooth, uneven edge however near you are, is dark crimson with a wet sheen when spilt, and dries brown and dull. `OwnTextures=false` and `Low` draw the game's own.
- In `Medium` and `High`, shots strike what they hit in first person. Brick, plaster and concrete throw out chips and a cloud of their dust, wood pale splinters, metal sparks, earth clods and a plume of dirt, snow and water a spray, glass glittering bits, and a body a mist and drops of blood, which the game's blood option set to none leaves out. What flies is the colour of what it came from, in the light it is in. Walls, floors and ceilings keep the hole: a crater in masonry, splinters lifted along the grain in wood, a bright ring in metal. The last 400 holes stay for as long as you play the save. Every firearm's shot leaves its mark, each pellet of a shotgun's and other players' shots on a server, and where a shot goes and what it hits are the game's own.
- Fire in first person, in `Medium` and `High`. A burning house, a Molotov's spill and a campfire have flames that lick up out of them and a column of smoke that rises and drifts with the wind, both growing and dying as the game's fire does; before, the view showed nothing there. A lit fireplace or cooking pit burns inside the mod's model of it, and a lit wood stove glows through its door's grille. The nearest fires you can see light what is round them with a flickering orange light. `OwnFire` in `[Content]` switches the mod's fire off, and the game's own flat flames and smoke are then stood up where they burn, as they are in `Low`.
- Your hands hold each firearm by its own grip. Where the right hand goes, the line it closes along, the fore-end the left hand is under, the magazine well, the slide, a bolt's knob and the loading port are read from the weapon's own model, so a pistol is held round its grip and not over its slide, and a gun another mod adds is held by its own grip. A handgun is held in both hands while your left hand is free, the left thumb forward along the frame. Each grip lies aslant across the palm, so your forearms come up to the weapon from under the picture and are not turned over. A firearm whose model has no `muzzle` attachment along its barrel or no trigger guard to find is held by the game's own arms.
- A melee attack is drawn in time with the game's own. The weapon is drawn back, comes round and lands on the frame the game lands the hit. What it lands on stops it and it springs back, and a miss goes on through. A swing, an overhead blow, an uppercut, a blow onto the ground, the thrust of a knife or a spear and a shove each have their own movement, and your shoulders turn with them. Each weapon is held and swung the way its kind is: an axe, a machete or a knife leads with its edge, a hammer or a club with its head and a frying pan with its face, your hands sit on the handle where the game's own do, a spear is held level with your left hand out along the shaft, anything too long to stand upright in the picture, a sledgehammer or a shovel, leans across you at rest, and a knife or a spear stabs point first into a zombie on the ground. A blow that lands holds where it struck for a moment, a knife's barely and a sledgehammer's clearly, then catches up with the game's swing; only the picture stops, and the hit and the next attack keep the game's timing. With no weapon in your hands a shove brings both hands up to push palms first, and a stomp braces your fists; a torch or another thing that is not a weapon stays in the hand that holds it and goes out and back with the shove, while a free hand pushes, and the shove shows your sleeves and gloves. A zombie shoved or reeling into your face is cut away round your eye, the cut closed in shade, instead of being drawn swollen and torn across the picture.
- Shots knock bodies about in first person. A zombie a shot drops goes into the game's ragdoll on the frame it is hit, where the game blended into it over a third of a second with the zombie still drawn standing, and the push goes along the line the round flew, from where it went in, so a head snaps back and a leg is knocked out. The legs give way under the body, which folds at the knees as it is thrown back, where it fell before as a stiff plank on its heels. A zombie that stays on its feet is jolted where the round went in, harder by a rifle or a shotgun than a pistol: a shot to the chest rocks the body back and one to the thigh kicks the leg out behind. The ragdolls need the game's Use Physics Hit Reactions option, which the game turns off on a server.
- A quick click attacks with a melee weapon as it does with a firearm: one the game saw held for a single frame is enough.
- The first person view goes on past the map the game has loaded. At 1920 by 1080 the game keeps about 75 tiles of map round you, and the view ended there in haze: from a rooftop twenty floors up there was little to see but the roof. Beyond it the land is now drawn for 768 tiles each way from the picture of its map the game ships, with a plain block for every building as high as its floors. Nothing else is drawn out there but trees (below): no vehicles, furniture or zombies.
- Vehicles lie on the first person hills. A car tips with the slope under its wheels, nose up on a climb and leaning on a bank, and you, its cabin, its passengers and its headlights tip with it. The hills are in the picture only: every vehicle drives as the game's level ground has it, alone and on a server.
- Played alone, a vehicle faster than a walk knocks down fences and street lamp posts and drives on. A fence breaks as the game breaks it when zombies bring it down, a lamp post goes with its lamp, whose light goes out, and the vehicle loses some speed and takes a crash's knock for each, a post more than a fence. A slow push stops at a fence as before, and on a server the game's own rules stand.
- Trees go on past the map the game has loaded, so that woods no longer appear out of nothing as you walk or drive up to them. Beyond the loaded map trees stand where the game's map puts them, of the kinds and sizes the game grows in each place, coloured as the trees round you are in the season, and the game's own trees take over as its map loads. They are the right trees for the place, not the game's own one for one. `FarTreeDistance` in `[Graphics]` sets how far, from 0 (none) to 768 tiles: 300 in `Low` and `Medium`, 768 in `High`.
- In first person a zombie is hidden by what stands between it and your eye, and by nothing else. The game fades a zombie in as your character comes to see the square it stands on: in first person that was a zombie appearing out of nothing a pace away as you came round a corner, and none at all past about thirty tiles in the open. Every zombie in front of the view is now drawn whole for as far as the game lets your character see in the weather and the light, on foot as from a vehicle.
- In first person your character sees no more than the game lets them. Fog, rain and snow are air in the picture: in the game's full fog the street is gone a few tiles off, as it is in the game's own view, and lighter fog, rain and snow hide what stands further off. The air takes the colour of the sky it lies under, and at night of the light on the ground round you, and in `High`, where the air is lit, fog glows where a torch or headlights shine through it. Past the distance the game lets your character see in the weather and at the hour, zombies fade out as the game fades them. Tiredness, panic, drink and some headwear darken the edge of the view as they narrow the game's cone of sight. What is behind your character is not dimmed: you turn your head to see it.
- In `Medium` and `High`, in first person a room is whole before you can see into it, near or far, and what is in a room you cannot see into is not drawn, nor anyone standing in it away from its doors and windows. The game shows a room once your character has had it in sight, which it works out a moment after they get there, and built by that a bathroom was empty when its door opened and its sink and toilet appeared with you standing in it. What is in a room is now built whether or not the game has shown it, for every room you could see into from about where you stand whichever way you turned, and every room behind a shut door or a drawn curtain within 10 tiles of you, so it is there the moment a door opens, you turn a corner, look in at a window or lean round a frame. It is drawn while a line of sight from your eye reaches into the room, through a doorway, an open door, a window, a door with glass in it or up a stairwell, and left out while every way in is shut, out of view, behind a wall or behind another building. Such a room is lit as the rooms in sight are until the game has a light for it. The far end of a street is also built before you turn to face it. On a server where players can fight, a room is still filled only as the game shows it to everyone.
- Lines painted on roads are drawn as paint. The game lays a road's centre lines, edge lines, stop bars, crossings, arrows and parking bays as small pictures, one a square, and seen from the ground each had a saw edge and stepped sideways at every square. First person now draws each as the shape it is, in the game's colour: straight edges and one width along a road, sharp from close by and clean far down it. It is so in `Medium` and `High`, and `OwnTextures=false` puts the game's pictures back, as `Low` has them.
- Pavements, car parks, forecourts and yards are laid, not bulged. A pavement along a shop front lies level at the shop's footing out to the far kerb, the road as high at one kerb as at the other, and past the end of the building comes back to the land as a gentle ramp, where on a hillside the square of paving against each wall climbed or dropped as much as a whole storey to the door. A row of shops with narrow passages between stands on one level, and a town's streets lose the swells of open country. In `Medium` and `High` the strip of concrete between a road and its pavement is drawn as paint is, of one width along the street with a clean edge.
- Real hills in the country. The land outside towns rises into hills of its own where the game's map has farmland or woods round about, more of them and higher than the gentle rise and fall elsewhere, up to about eight tiles, with the steepest main road over them climbing about one in eight. There are none in a town, and like the rest they fall away to water. `HillHeight` (`Hill height` in the game's options) sets how high every hill and swell of the first person land stands, from `0`, as level as the game's map, to `2`. It is `1.5` unless you change it. A kerb, a bank down to water and how far water lies below its banks stay as they are at every height, and on a server where players can fight it stays at `1`.
- Graphics modes for first person and the isometric view. `F9` goes to the next mode and sets both views to it, names it on the screen and saves it, as `Quality` for first person and `IsometricQuality` for the isometric view. The game's options set the two apart, and switching views names the mode the new view is drawn in. First person starts in `Automatic` and the isometric view in `Low`, the game's own picture, so the picture steps up as you press `Delete`. The isometric view has `Low`, `Medium`, `High` and `Custom`, and no `Automatic`: `F9` leaves it in `Low` while first person is in `Automatic`, and skips `Automatic` when pressed in the isometric view. `Custom`, after `High`, draws the mix the thirteen rows of `[Graphics]` set, a row for each thing the other modes decide. The game's options show those rows as the mode you are in draws them, and changing any of them there, or in the file while the game runs, goes straight to `Custom`. `Low` leaves the game's isometric view as the game draws it and draws first person from the game's own sprites, models and textures, at a little over three fifths of the pixels each way and seeing 48 tiles. `Medium` and `High` draw both views with what the mod made, the isometric view on ground as level as the game's map with no kerbs, so a body or anything else lying in a road is where the game shows it and highlights it. `High` has everything on, frame blending included. `Medium` draws at four fifths of the pixels each way, with smaller shadow maps, far trees to 300 tiles, and without the shading of corners, the lit air, the reflections and the depth in near surfaces. `TreeQuality` sets the trees: `Low` is the game's sprites, `Medium` (in `Medium`) the mod's trees within 40 tiles and the game's sprites past them, `High` the mod's trees as far as you see. `Automatic`, which `F9` comes to after `Custom`, begins as `High`, draws less each time the first person picture falls under 45 frames a second for three seconds, down to `Low`'s own look, and draws more again a level at a time once it has held 57 or more for fifteen seconds, waiting longer each time a level it went back up to was too slow. Under a frame limit set in the game's options both rates are taken from the limit. It says each step on the screen, as `Graphics: Automatic (Medium)`, and never changes a mode you chose yourself. In `Low` and `Medium`, people and zombies further off than 4 tiles throw the shadow of their bodies alone, without what they wear and carry, which costs less in a crowd.
- A draw distance for what stands in the world, apart from how far the world is built. `ZombiesAndPropsDrawDistance` in `[Graphics]`, a slider on the game's options screen from 16 to 80 tiles, is how far off zombies, people, vehicles and the furniture and decor the mod has models of are drawn in first person. Ahead of a vehicle you ride in they are drawn as far as `ViewDistance`, which also sets how far the land, the buildings and the plants go, and the draw distance never reaches past it. Each mode draws them as far as its own view goes. On a server where players can hurt each other nothing is left out.
- `Reflections` in `[Graphics]`, a tick box of its own on the options screen. Off, wet roads, polished floors and glass mirror nothing, water shows the sky alone as in `Low` and `Medium`, and a vehicle's mirrors are plain glass with the road behind them not drawn at all. On, a vehicle's mirrors show the road behind in every mode, and the rest mirrors where `ShadingAndLight` is on, as in `High`. `ShadingAndLight` is the shaded corners and the lit air.
- In `Medium` and `High` the isometric view opens a building as the game does. The floors over yours are left out of the buildings the game leaves them out of, your own and one that stands between the camera and you, the walls the game cuts away are cut down to a low course, and a room is as light as the game has it: dark until your character has seen into it, and dimmer while they cannot see it.
- In `Medium` and `High`, content the mod made in place of the game's own: models of the pump action shotgun, the sawn-off pump shotgun, the hunting rifle and rifle scopes, of the three pistols, of the patrol revolver, the magnum, the snub-nosed revolver and the toy cap pistol (on each shot the hammer falls and the cylinder turns a chamber; to reload, the cylinder swings out, the empties drop out and the rounds go in one by one as the game loads them), of the over and under double barrelled shotgun and the same gun sawn off, which share one set of pictures (a shot fires the lower barrel first; to reload, the top lever goes over and the barrels drop open, the fired shells are thrown out back over your shoulder, the new shells go in and the gun is snapped shut, as the game loads it), furniture, banners and bunting hung as cloth, a moving picture on a television that is switched on, bars with nothing coming in and with a channel or a tape the show that is on, cut from shot to shot, pictures on walls, some of the lettered signs on shops and other walls with the wording the game's own carry, the flag hung on a wall, plants, surface textures, built windows, doors, curtains and handrails, skins for zombies, for the living and for animals, and what people wear: the game's own picture of each garment, head of hair and beard enlarged with its outline smoothed and the weave, knit, grain or strands of what it is made of laid over it, and T-shirt prints, blood and dirt of the mod's own. Each is a row of `[Content]` in `CameraUnlock.ini`, eleven in all, `true` unless you change it. Set a row to `false` and the game's own is drawn there, as the game or another mod has it.
- Animals near you in `Medium` and `High` wear skins of the mod's own, eight times as wide as the game's for cattle, pigs, sheep and deer and more for the smaller ones: cows and bulls of each of the game's three breeds, Holstein patched black and white, Simmental red with a white face and Angus black, with a coat of short hair, a bare nose with its nostrils, eyes and hooves; pigs pink and black; sheep with a fleece and a black or a pale face; deer with a white belly, throat and eye rings, a spotted fawn and a buck's antlers; hens, cockerels and chicks; turkeys; rabbits, a raccoon with its mask and ringed tail, rats and mice. The game drew a cow in its own skin of 256 dots across, however near you stood. A skinned or rotting carcass wears the game's own skin. `OwnAnimals=false` draws the game's own skins.
- Arms and hands of the mod's own on a held weapon, in every graphics mode, `Low` included, that close on it and work its bolt, slide, magazine and loading port. The game's own arms, moved with the weapon to before the eye, hung across the middle of the picture. `OwnArms=false`, `Own arms` in the game's options, draws the game's own arms in every mode, for another mod that changes the arms or the weapons.
- In `Medium` and `High`, the game's x2, x4 and x8 scopes are drawn as a scope you look through. Its eyepiece shows the world at the scope's power with the reticle in it, and the picture is whole only with your eye behind the scope: off its axis or too far back, a shadow closes in. Lean in to the eyepiece and the picture grows with it.
- Snow that has settled is a surface of its own in `Medium` and `High`, in first person and in the isometric view. It lies in swells, lumps and wind ripples, ends in a rounded lip where the ground shows through, is dimmer and bluer in its hollows and glitters in the sun, and less of it lies under a tree or the eaves of a house. It banks up against a kerb in a soft slope out into the street, broader in one place than the next, where the kerb stood out of it as a hard step. It keeps your latest footprints, which in first person point the way you face however you step, and a rut behind each wheel of the vehicle you drive, so on a bend the front tracks swing wide of the back ones, until the snow is gone. In `Low` it is the game's own plain white, with no shape, footprints or glitter.
- Trees, bushes and other plants carry the snow that lies, as much as the game says lies: on the upper side of a crown's sprigs and its boughs, as caps and clumps on a bush or a plant drawn from the game's own picture, along the top of a limb and up the weather side of a bole, and caught in the ivy. With a little snow lying they have a dusting, and with deep snow they are heavy with it. The snow on a plant is lit as the snow on the ground beside it is, and a bough laden with it swings less in the wind. Grass stands in the snow as deep as the snow lies at its root, bent over by the snow on it, and stands clear of it under a tree or the eaves where the ground shows.
- In `Medium` and `High`, plants first person still drew as the game's flat pictures, set up crossed through each other, stand as the trees and bushes round them do. Pines, hemlocks and hollies have a trunk and a crown of needles, and the game's dead trees have limbs, branches and twigs of bark. The leafy wild plants are bushes, the game's ferns are ferns, and fallen sticks lie on the ground where they stood on end. Dried corn and the dead leafy crops in the fields grow stalk by stalk with their leaves, corn with its tassel, within 30 tiles of you, and are the game's pictures past that. Ivy that climbs a tree also spreads over the ground round its foot. `OwnPlants=false` and `Low` draw the game's own.
- In autumn, leaves come off the broad-leaved trees round you in first person, in `Medium` and `High`: a few while the crowns turn, most as they thin, none once they are bare, and none from pines, hemlocks, hollies or dead trees. Each is the colour its tree has turned, flutters, swings and spins as it falls, and is carried by the wind: on a still day a few drift down, in a gale many more go and travel far. They lie a few seconds where they land and fade into the leaf on the ground. `OwnPlants=false` and `Low` have none.
- Lightning in first person, in `Medium` and `High`. Where the game's storm strikes, a bolt comes down out of the cloud: a faint leader feels its way to the ground, the first stroke lights the whole crooked channel and its branches white, and it strikes again down the same path two or three times more before it goes out. For those moments it is the light of the world: the cloud it came from glows from inside, the sky and the rain light up, and what stands in the open is lit from the bolt's side and throws a shadow away from it. Thunder that comes with no strike flickers through the clouds. The game's thunder is heard as before, later the further off the strike. The game's option to turn lightning off in storms turns all of it off.
- Three picture styles, cycled on `F8`, named on the screen and saved as `Style`: `Vivid`, the default, `Natural` and `Bleak`. A style changes how the mod's picture is developed from the light in it and nothing else.
- Up to four players at one screen each have a first person view of their own. `Delete` tapped switches the first player's view, tapped twice the second player's, and held for 0.4 seconds every player's. Players 2, 3 and 4 have their head trackers listened for on UDP ports `4243`, `4244` and `4245` (`SecondPlayerUdpPort`, `ThirdPlayerUdpPort`, `FourthPlayerUdpPort`), the first player's on `4242` as before. On a controller in first person the aiming stick turns the view, the right trigger attacks, and with a firearm in hand the left trigger raises the sights. The tracking, yaw, aim and graphics modes and the picture style are shared by every player. Each player's character turns its head with its own player's.
- The first player can play first person on a controller. The aiming stick turns the view, the movement stick walks the way it faces, the right trigger attacks and with a firearm in hand the left trigger raises the sights, and the mouse is left alone. Back tapped twice on a controller switches its player's first person view, for the first player and the second alike. The first player plays on whatever last worked the main menu, a controller that pressed A there or the keyboard and mouse once clicked or typed on, and any other controller pressed once the game is under way joins as the next player.
- A controller plays when the game's Controller options has it ticked. While none of the gamepads plugged in is ticked, every gamepad the game recognises is ticked and plays, where the game ticked only the first device it found, which with a 3Dconnexion SpaceMouse plugged in left no controller working. A 3Dconnexion device is never taken as a controller.
- Two players at one screen can be one above the other. The game puts them side by side, each with half the width. `F7` goes between that and player 1 above player 2, each with the whole width and half the height, names on the screen which it went to and saves it as `SplitScreen` (`Vertical` or `Horizontal`), which is read again while the game runs. With one, three or four players the screen is as the game lays it out and the key does nothing.
- `FieldOfView` in `CameraUnlock.ini` sets the first person field of view, in degrees from the bottom of the picture to the top: `65` by default, from `40` to `110`. It is read again while the game runs, as are `Quality`, `Style` and the rows of `[Graphics]` and `[Content]`, so a change shows without a restart.
- A flashlight you carry follows your head and not your aim. `LightMultiplier` sets how far it turns for each degree your head turns.
- A flashlight you carry throws a beam in the mod's view: a bright middle with a dim spill round it, which stops at whatever stands in it and lays that thing's shadow on what is behind. It lights walls, floors, ceilings, furniture, zombies, other people, rain and snow alike, and reaches up a staircase to the floor above before you are on it. The game's own way of lighting a flashlight, every floor tile in a wide fan on the level you stand on, is left out of the picture and still decides what your character can see and who can see them.
- Multiplayer. The first person view and head tracking work when you join a server, and only you need the mod: the server and the other players do not. Other players' names and speech are written over their heads in the first person view. Your character's head turns with yours on your own screen only.
- Fair play where players can hurt each other, which is any server with PvP on. There a lean does not move a shot: with the sights up the lean across the aim eases out and the round leaves from your character. Walking sideways or backwards puts your character in the game's own aiming stance, so they face the way the view does.
- Settings are in `CameraUnlock.ini` in the game folder, which the mod creates when it starts and finds none. A setting left at `default` follows `Defaults.ini`, shared with other head tracking mods that keep their settings in a `CameraUnlock.ini`. Every key above is a row of `[Hotkeys]` and can be moved to other keys or left empty. A key pressed in the game's main menu, or while you are typing in one of its text boxes, does nothing.
- The mod's settings in the game's own options. The `Mods` tab has a `Head Tracking` section with the tracking mode, the aim mode, the yaw mode, the field of view, the graphics mode, the picture style, frame blending, how two players split the screen and each row of `[Content]`. What you apply there is saved to `CameraUnlock.ini` and takes effect at once, and the section shows the current settings each time the options open.
- An installer with no mod loader. `install.cmd` copies `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.jar` and `CameraUnlockCore.dll` next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe` and writes `ProjectZomboid64.site.json` from the game's own launch settings, so the game's Java runtime loads the mod. It finds a Steam copy of the game by itself and takes any other copy as a path. `uninstall.cmd` leaves `CameraUnlock.ini` in place.
- Each save starts in the game's own view. Head tracking moves the view while the game is not paused and your character is alive.
- The mod follows one tracker at a time and listens on IPv4. If a second program sends to the port, the mod stays with the first until that one has been silent for two seconds, and the log names both.
- If a game update changes something the mod uses, the mod stays out and the game runs without it. `ProjectZomboidHeadTracking.log`, next to `ProjectZomboid64.exe`, names what changed.
